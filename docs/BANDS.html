Combine · difficulty-band slicing

How a theme's recipes get cut into discovery-difficulty bands — numeric tiers indicating how reachable a recipe is from the base pool, not temporal "ages" — pushed forward to satisfy dependencies, and merged until every surviving band is thick enough to play. The identifiers deriveNumAges / assignAges are historical; the concept they implement is difficulty, not time. Source: src/combine/data.ts (line 83 and line 116).

Reading the badges · item-level vs. recipe-level

The animation shows two different levels, and they're easy to conflate.

Item level · the badge on a tile

A per-item depth. In data.ts it lives in the d map inside assignAges (line 126).

  • Theme ingredients the theme doesn't make (Wheat, Water, Milk) get their computeRefine() value from the knowledge base. In our mock everything raw starts at 0.
  • Theme outputs (Flour, Dough, Bread) start at ∞ and are solved down to 1 + max(part item-levels), taking the min across all recipes that produce them. If Butter had two recipes with recipe-levels 1 and 2, Butter's item level would be 1.

Recipe level · the lvl column

A per-recipe value, stored on CombineRecipe.lvl and assigned at data.ts:164 as r.lvl = 1 + max(item-level of each part).

  • This is the value the band cut reads. The histogram the slicer walks is a histogram of recipe-levels.
  • For a theme output with exactly one recipe, item-level(out) === recipe-level. That's why in our mock the badge on the output tile always matches the number in the lvl column — every item has exactly one recipe.
So why does Wheat's badge read "0"? Not because of any recipe — Wheat has none in this theme. 0 is its KB refine value. Flour's badge reads "1" because 1 + max(0, 0) = 1 for the Flour recipe (Wheat + Water → Flour), and Flour has no cheaper alternative. Bread's badge reads "3" because the Bread recipe is Dough(2) + Yeast(0) → Bread, i.e. 1 + 2.
Where they can diverge. If an item has multiple recipes, the item-level is the min across them while each recipe keeps its own lvl. The slicer bands recipes by r.lvl, so an item with a cheap recipe and an expensive one can appear in the cheap-recipe's band even though an expensive recipe still exists at a later band. The push-forward pass later lifts the recipe if needed, but outBand (the per-item min band) tracks whichever recipe first produces the item.

In this toy KB the band sizes (3, 2, 1) are well below MIN_BAND_SIZE = 10, so a real run of the merge pass would collapse to a single age — MIN_EFFECTIVE_AGES caps that at 3 when enough bands are populated. We've skipped the merge here to keep the example readable; the "sparse-merge" diagram further down shows how it would actually fire on a production-sized theme.

1 · How many bands?

deriveNumAges(recipeCount, prevNumAges) picks a target band count by three forces:

  • Volume: floor(recipes / 25)
  • Floor: MIN_AGES = 3
  • Sparsity cap: floor(recipes / 3) — don't fabricate ages a tiny KB can't fill
  • Monotonic: result ≥ prevNumAges (leaderboard seasons can't retroactively collapse)
Why monotonic. If an admin deletes recipes, the band count stays put. Past runs on five bands keep their meaning even if today's KB would only justify four.

2–3 · Levels, then a balanced cut

Each recipe gets a numeric lvl — one plus the max level of its parts, saturating theme outputs that depend on later-made items. Then the sorted level histogram is walked and cut so each band holds a similar recipe count.

Three gates on every candidate cut. (1) refuse if cutting would leave more future bands than remaining levels; (2) prefer cuts where the running size hits the target; (3) force a cut when levels-left equals bands-after-cut — otherwise early misses lock us out of ever cutting (the Nov 2026 band-collapse bug).

4 · Push forward

After the cut, a recipe might land in band 2 while one of its parts is only produced in band 3 — unreachable in order. The push-forward loop bumps each recipe up to the highest band that produces any of its parts, recomputes, and repeats until stable (up to 20 passes).

Dough (b2) + Yeast (b3) → Bread (b2)
→
Dough (b2) + Yeast (b3) → Bread (b3)

Bread's band lifts from 2 → 3 so Yeast is already craftable by the time the recipe appears.

5 · Merge sparse bands

Push-forward tends to gut whichever band exported its outputs upward. Any band with fewer than MIN_BAND_SIZE = 10 recipes becomes a "dead age" — the dealer can't surface enough current-age combos for the player to progress. The merge pass folds each sparse band into a neighbour until every surviving band passes the floor, then renumbers.

Merge direction. Band X>1 folds down into X-1. Band 1 (no predecessor) folds up into band 2. Either way the relative order of parts and outputs is unchanged, so the "no later-age dependency" invariant from push-forward still holds.
Floor. Merging never drops below MIN_EFFECTIVE_AGES = 3 (or the populated-band count, if the KB really is that small). A thin top age is better UX than collapsing to a single age with no visible progression.

How recipes get sampled · the dealer

Band assignment decides which recipes are reachable at each age. The dealer picks the actual tiles the player sees in the tray from that reachable set, one tile at a time, three tiles per tray. Lives in src/combine/deal.ts.

Per-age pool · ageSets(bundle, age)

Before any dealing, the engine computes the pool of recipes playable at this age:

  • dealBands = { age, age + 1 } — the current band and the one just below (0-based → 1-based k - 1, k). Recipes in these two bands make up A.recipes, the set the dealer draws from.
  • useBands = { age - 1, age, age + 1 } — a wider halo. Tiles whose recipe sits in these bands won't crumble, so a leftover from the previous age stays useful for one more age before expiring.
  • A.ING — every ingredient id appearing in dealBands recipes. A.BASE — ingredients that aren't themselves theme outputs (raw stuff + tools). A.MID — ingredients that are made things (chains start here).
Why both current and previous band? If the dealer only served current-band recipes, Age-II tiles would show up the instant the player ages up with no overlap. Including age - 1 keeps a short runway of still-useful older items in play — the "age transition" feels like a shift, not a reset.

Pick a tile · pickOne() layered strategies

Each tile is picked independently by rolling roll ∈ [0, 1) and falling through a cascade. The percentages below are the roll bands; higher-priority layers eat later ones when they hit.

layer -1
Diversify escape
Pathological pile detected across last 2 trays? First slot picks from A.BASE but skips any ingredient in the pile tile's recipe graph.
layer 0
Pile partner
If a tile is piling on the board (count ≥ 2), roll in [0.45, 0.45 + w) picks a partner from a recipe that uses the piled tile. Window w is 0.15 / 0.30 / 0.45 for pile sizes 2 / 3 / 4+.
layer 1
Fits now
roll < 0.45. Pick any ingredient from A.ING that would combine on some empty cell with the current board. First-order match-maker.
layer 2
Partner on board
roll < 0.80. Something that pairs with a tile already on the board, even if it won't combine immediately. Pile-touching recipes weighted 3× over generic partners.
layer 3+4
Chain seeds & raw
roll < 0.88: a made thing from A.MID (next move can start a chain). Else fall through to A.BASE raw ingredients; ~25% of the time allow tools too.

Fill the tray · dealTray()

Builds a 3-tile tray by calling pickOne three times, then gates on reachability. Each slot sees the prior slot in the tray so it doesn't pick a duplicate.

  1. Flip wantOne (75% true): "does this tray need a first-placement combine, or is a two-placement setup OK?"
  2. Up to 60 attempts: pick 3 tiles, reject if they repeat an obvious pattern (duplicate tile in first 40 tries; a recent tray key in first 40 tries).
  3. Accept the tray when playsInOne is true and wantOne, or when playsInTwo is true and !wantOne. A playsInTwo tray is kept as a fallback for use if we never find a wantOne.
  4. Last-resort: hand over both halves of a random 2-ingredient recipe plus one picked tile, then shuffle slots so the "answer" isn't always slots 0 and 1.
Guarantees that drop out of this. Simulation shows ≥98% of trays on empty boards have a play within two placements (see src/combine/__tests__/deal.test.ts). The last-resort path makes the guarantee hard, not statistical — if every random attempt fails, you still get a 2-recipe straight away.

What the dealer deliberately doesn't do

  • No weighting toward rare recipes. Every reachable recipe in A.recipes is equally available to the layered picks — the shaping is all around board state, not recipe scarcity.
  • No peek at madeAtBand or found. The dealer doesn't know what the player has or hasn't discovered this run. That's by design: discovery is a side-effect of play, not a goal the dealer steers toward.
  • No band-up pre-staging. The dealer never deals an age + 2 tile to "prepare" the player for the next age. The pool is strictly {age - 1, age} for picking.

Invariants a correct slicing holds

  • Every recipe lands in exactly one band in [1 .. effective].
  • For every recipe r in band b, every part of r is produced by some recipe in band ≤ b.
  • Every surviving band holds at least MIN_BAND_SIZE = 10 recipes, unless the MIN_EFFECTIVE_AGES floor is forcing a thin band.
  • Band numbers are contiguous 1..M with no gaps after merge.
